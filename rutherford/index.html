Think about it
1Where do most particles end up? What does that tell you about the inside of the gold foil?
2Some particles change direction. What could be inside the foil that pushes them sideways?
3A few particles bounce almost straight back. How heavy or solid would something need to be to do that?
4Change the nucleus size and run again. How do the results change? What does that tell you about the size of the real nucleus?
Radium source & lead block click to expand▼

Radium-226 decay chain (Wikimedia Commons)

A small amount of radium sits inside a thick lead block. Radium is radioactive — it shoots alpha particles in all directions. The dense lead absorbs them all except those escaping through a narrow slit. Alpha particles are helium nuclei: 2 protons + 2 neutrons — exactly what you see flying across the screen!

Detector screen click to expand▼

Expected vs. actual results (Wikimedia Commons)

The blue arc is a circular screen coated with zinc sulfide (ZnS). Each alpha particle that hits it produces a tiny flash of light — a scintillation. Geiger and Marsden counted these flashes by hand in a dark room! Most appeared straight ahead, but a few at large angles changed our understanding of the atom forever.
